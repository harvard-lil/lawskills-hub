# Profile-Specific Resume Guidance

Different student profiles face distinct resume challenges. After identifying the student's profile in Step 2, load the relevant section below for profile-specific evaluation criteria.

A student may match more than one profile (e.g., a 2L with military experience). Apply guidance from all relevant sections.

## 1L / No Prior Work Experience

Students coming to law school directly from undergraduate or a master's program with no full-time professional work experience.

### Key Challenges

- Sparse Experience section that looks thin compared to peers with work history.
- Tendency to undervalue student activities, part-time jobs, and volunteer work.

### Guidance

- **Leverage student organizations as Experience entries.** A leadership role in an a cappella group, student government, admissions office, or campus publication can be listed under Experience (not just Education Activities) with full bullet-point treatment -- but only if the student can describe substantive responsibilities.
- **When an activity appears under Experience, remove it from Education Activities** to avoid duplication.
- **Multi-position formatting** is useful: if the student held multiple roles in the same organization (e.g., Business Manager then President), list both under one employer entry to show growth.
- **Part-time and campus jobs count.** Tutoring, daycare, restaurant work, retail -- all demonstrate transferable skills (client management, multitasking, attention to detail, communication).
- **Quantify wherever possible.** "Coordinated performances for 15-member group" and "Surpassed budget expectations by 24% ($3,650)" are much stronger than "Managed group logistics."
- **First law school activities matter.** Even 1L fall semester activities (reading groups, student org memberships) can appear if the student has space and the activities are substantive.

### Common Mistakes

- Listing too many minor honors. If *summa cum laude* is listed, individual semester Dean's List entries are redundant.
- Leaving the Experience section underdeveloped because the student thinks only "real jobs" count.

## Prior Professional Work Experience

Students who worked full-time before law school (consulting, finance, government, engineering, teaching, military, nonprofit, etc.).

### Key Challenges

- Fitting substantial pre-law careers onto one page.
- Translating non-legal work into language legal employers value.
- Deciding how many bullets to allocate to older vs. newer positions.

### Guidance

- **Entry length should be proportional to recency and relevance.** The most recent and legally relevant positions get 3-5 bullets. Older or less relevant positions may get 1-2 bullets or even a single descriptive line.
- **Lead with the most substantive bullet.** The first bullet in each entry gets the most attention -- place the responsibility or achievement you most want to discuss there.
- **Frame pre-law work in terms legal employers value.** Consulting experience translates to: client advisory, document review, regulatory analysis, stakeholder management. Finance translates to: due diligence, compliance, quantitative analysis. Teaching translates to: communication of complex material, assessment, curriculum development.
- **Include metrics.** "Conducted document review for nearly a dozen ongoing cases" and "Led qualitative and quantitative research including stakeholder interviews" demonstrate scope.
- **Harvard Law Entrepreneurship Project, clinics, and research positions** can bridge the gap between pre-law career and legal career trajectory. Feature them prominently if the student participated.
- **Certifications** (e.g., CMLA, CPA, PE) are worth including if relevant to the target employer or practice area.

### Common Mistakes

- Allocating equal space to every position regardless of recency.
- Using industry jargon that legal employers will not understand without translation.
- Omitting the HLS clinical or experiential work that connects the pre-law career to the law degree.

## LL.M. Candidates

Students from foreign jurisdictions pursuing an LL.M. at HLS.

### Key Challenges

- Presenting foreign legal education and credentials in a format U.S. employers understand.
- Demonstrating language capabilities and cross-jurisdictional knowledge.
- Addressing bar membership and admission plans.

### Guidance

- **Education section:** List "LL.M. Candidate, May [year]" with a "Focus: [area]" line (e.g., "Focus: Corporate and Banking Law").
- **Foreign law degree:** List as "LL.B." with the institution name and country. Include class rank if available and impressive (e.g., "Ranked 5 in a class of 400").
- **Thesis:** Include if substantive and relevant. Be prepared to discuss it.
- **Named awards from foreign institutions** need parenthetical explanations (e.g., "Fritz Meyer Award (best grade in Constitutional Law)").
- **Professional Memberships section:** Include bar admissions and planned bar examinations (e.g., "Licensed to practice law in [Country]; will sit for New York Bar in July [year]").
- **Languages section should be prominent.** List all languages with proficiency levels. For LL.M. candidates this is often a significant asset.
- **Experience section:** Frame foreign legal experience (associate work, judicial clerkships, research positions) in terms U.S. employers understand. "Counseled clients on regulatory compliance, corporate governance, and strategic business ventures" is clear. Jurisdiction-specific terminology may need brief explanation.
- **Exchange programs** during the foreign LL.B. can be listed under Education.

### Common Mistakes

- Using degree titles or honors systems that U.S. employers do not recognize without explanation.
- Omitting bar admission status and plans.
- Underselling language skills that are a genuine competitive advantage.

## Military Experience

Students with active-duty military service.

### Key Challenges

- Translating military rank, titles, and accomplishments into civilian language.
- Balancing extensive military experience with limited page space.
- Demonstrating how military leadership maps to legal skills.

### Guidance

- **List military positions under Experience** with the branch, location(s), rank, and role title.
- **Multiple positions within the same branch** can be listed as sub-entries under one employer heading (e.g., three roles under "United States Marine Corps") to save space and show career progression.
- **Translate military language.** "Commanded 46 Marines and Navy corpsmen in female engagement operations" is accessible. Avoid acronyms and jargon unless defined.
- **Awards and decorations** (e.g., "Navy/Marine Corps Commendation Medal for meritorious service") should be included as bullet points within the relevant position entry.
- **Quantify scope:** number of personnel commanded, number of briefings delivered, geographic scope, operational impact.
- **Connect to legal skills:** planning, coordination, supervision, drafting standard operating procedures, briefing senior leadership, managing complex logistics -- these all translate directly.
- **Deployment locations and combat experience** can be listed factually. They demonstrate resilience, adaptability, and high-stakes decision-making.

### Common Mistakes

- Assuming the reader understands military acronyms, rank structures, or operational terminology.
- Giving equal space to all military roles rather than emphasizing the most senior or substantive.
- Omitting the transition context (why law school after military service) -- this is better addressed in a cover letter, but the resume's strategic ordering can imply the narrative.

## 2L / 3L Students

Upper-level students who have completed 1L summer positions and have more law school activities to showcase.

### Key Challenges

- Deciding between one-address and two-address format.
- Incorporating 1L summer experience and expanded law school activities.
- Balancing legal and pre-law experience when space is tight.

### Guidance

- **One address vs. two addresses:**
  - **One address** (school city/state, centered or left-justified): default format. Use when applying broadly or when home market is not relevant.
  - **Two addresses** (school and home): use when applying in or near the home market to signal geographic ties. Format as "School: Cambridge, MA | Home: Los Angeles, CA."
- **1L summer experience** should appear prominently in Experience, typically as the first or second entry.
- **Journal and clinic roles** often expand by 2L/3L -- update titles (e.g., Subciter becomes Technical Editor or Articles Editor).
- **Older pre-law experience may be compressed** to make room for legal experience. A position that had 4 bullets as a 1L might be reduced to 2 bullets as a 3L.
- **Dean's Scholar Prizes** earned in 1L courses should be added to HLS Honors.
- **Research assistantships** for professors can appear under Experience or Activities depending on scope.

### Common Mistakes

- Not updating the resume to reflect the shift from pre-law to legal experience emphasis.
- Keeping old formatting and bullet counts from the 1L version without rebalancing.
- Listing a home address when not applying to that market (wastes space and confuses the reader).

## Visiting and Transfer Students

Students whose home institution is not HLS but who are studying at HLS, or who transferred to HLS from another law school.

### Key Challenges

- Listing two law school institutions clearly.
- Presenting honors and activities from the home institution alongside HLS.

### Guidance

- **List both institutions in Education.** The home law school keeps its original entry; HLS is added.
- **For visiting students:** List the home institution first (with degree candidacy) and HLS second as "Visiting Student, Academic year [year]." Activities at HLS go under the HLS entry.
- **For transfer students:** List HLS first (as the degree-granting institution) with a note about transfer status. Consult the OCS Transfer Students page for specific formatting.
- **Honors from the home institution** (e.g., law review membership, teaching assistant selection) should appear under that institution's entry.
- **STEM backgrounds** (chemistry, engineering, biology) are particularly valuable for patent law employers -- make sure technical coursework, research, and skills are visible.
- **Study abroad or exchange programs** during undergrad or the home law school can appear as their own entry or as a sub-line.

### Common Mistakes

- Omitting the home institution or making it unclear where the degree will be conferred.
- Not leveraging a technical undergraduate degree when targeting IP or patent law employers.
- Failing to list activities and honors under the correct institution.
