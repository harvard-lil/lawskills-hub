# HLS OCS Resume Standards

This reference distills the authoritative resume guidance from the Harvard Law School Office of Career Services (OCS). Use it as the evaluation baseline for any HLS student resume.

Source: HLS OCS "Resumes for the Private Sector" page and the 1L Resume Tutorial.

## Context: How Resumes Are Read

- First-round resume reviewers spend 5-10 seconds scanning a resume. AI screening may also be involved.
- Interviewers skim the resume during conversation, looking for specific talking points.
- The resume is a marketing tool. Its sole purpose is to persuade the reader to call the student for an interview.

## General Formatting

- **One page only.** No exceptions.
- **Font size:** 10.5pt minimum (10-11pt range). Name can be 14pt.
- **Margins:** 0.5 inches minimum on all sides.
- **Consistency is paramount:** font, bullet style, spacing, alignment, capitalization, italics, and date formatting must be uniform throughout.
- **Simplicity:** Clean lines, symmetry. No fancy graphics, colors, or decorative elements.
- **Print-friendly:** Adequate margins so nothing is cut off when printed.
- **No objective or personal summary statement.**

## Header

- **Name:** Can be larger than body text. Nicknames in parentheses or quotes are acceptable. Pronouns are optional.
- **Email:** School email only (e.g., `name@jd28.law.harvard.edu`). Never a personal email (Gmail, Yahoo, etc.). Remove hyperlink formatting.
- **Phone:** Include cell number. Ensure voicemail is professional.
- **Address:** City and state only. No full street address. A second "home" city is appropriate when signaling ties to a regional market.
- English is assumed; do not list it as a language.

## Education Section

### Order and Structure

- Reverse chronological: law school first, then graduate school (if any), then undergraduate. **No high school.**
- For each institution: institution name and location on one line; degree, honors, major, and graduation date on the next.

### Degree Formatting

- Use "Juris Doctor" -- never "Juris Doctorate."
- J.D. can appear as "J.D. Candidate, May 2028" or "Juris Doctor, Expected May 2028." If using "Candidate," do not also say "Expected."
- **Match degree abbreviation style across institutions.** If using "J.D.," use "B.A." (not "Bachelor of Arts"). If using "Juris Doctor," use "Bachelor of Arts."
- List graduation date only, not attendance dates (for law school).

### Honors

- Latin honors are **italicized and lowercase**: *magna cum laude*, *summa cum laude*, *cum laude*.
- **Phi Beta Kappa is NOT italicized** (it is an organization, not a Latin honor). List it under Honors.
- Dean's Scholar Prizes go under HLS Honors.
- Named prizes and awards should include a brief parenthetical explanation if the name is not self-explanatory (e.g., "Hyman S. Kleinman Memorial Prize (departmental thesis honors)").
- Limit honors to the most significant. Do not list every semester's Dean's List if a Latin honor already conveys the same information.

### Activities

- Journal names are **italicized**; positions/roles are not.
- Student organization names are not italicized.
- Leadership roles should be highlighted.
- List activities on separate lines, not crammed into a single bullet.

### GPA and Test Scores

- **HLS does not have GPAs.** Do not attempt to calculate one.
- **Do not include undergraduate GPA** unless the institution does not award Latin honors.
- **Never include LSAT scores or any standardized test scores.**

### Thesis

- Including a thesis title is optional. If included, be prepared to discuss it in interviews.

### Study Abroad

- Can be listed as its own entry under the college or as a one-line note (e.g., "Study Abroad: Sciences Po, Paris (spring 2023)").
- No need to list coursework unless it is substantively different and relevant.

## Experience Section

### Order and Structure

- Reverse chronological order.
- Includes: paid, volunteer, intern, military, and clinical positions.
- For each: employer name and location on one line; job title and dates on a second line.

### Dates

- During education: use season (e.g., "Summer 2024") or month/year (e.g., "May 2024 - Aug. 2024").
- Full-time positions: month/year ranges.
- If abbreviating months, use correct Bluebook abbreviations.
- Be consistent with date formatting throughout.

### Bullets

- **3-5 bullets per position.** Entry length should be proportional to recency and duration.
- Begin each bullet with a **strong action verb.** Use present tense for current positions, past tense for past positions.
- **No personal pronouns** (no "I," "my," "me").
- **Limit articles** (a, an, the) to improve conciseness.
- **No periods at the end of bullets** (be consistent either way, but OCS samples omit them).
- Avoid hanging lines (a single word wrapping to the next line) -- tighten language to prevent them.

### Content Quality

- **Specificity over vagueness.** Compare: "Wrote memoranda for litigation department" (weak) vs. "Drafted memorandum on attorney-client privilege where in-house counsel conducts sexual harassment investigation" (strong).
- **Emphasize transferable skills**: research, writing, analysis, leadership, communication, organization, project management, interpersonal skills.
- **Include metrics and quantifiable results** where possible (number of cases, dollar amounts, team sizes, percentage improvements).
- **Orient the reader**: if the employer or role is not well-known, briefly contextualize the industry or work.
- **First bullet gets the most attention** -- place the most substantive or impressive responsibility there.
- **Include substantive keywords** relevant to legal employers (document review, regulatory compliance, due diligence, memorandum drafting, etc.).
- Avoid vague verbs: "Helped," "Assisted with," "Supported" are weak. Be specific about what you actually did.
- Define acronyms and jargon that a legal employer may not know.
- For law-related work: indicate type of practice, substantive areas of law, and types of legal documents produced or handled.

### What Counts as Experience

- Teaching, tutoring, restaurant work, research, government, military, volunteer, student organizations -- all count.
- Student activities (e.g., a cappella president, admissions tour guide) can appear in Experience when the student lacks professional work history. When listed under Experience, do not also list them under Education Activities.

## Optional Sections

### Languages

- List each language with proficiency level in parentheses: "French (proficient)," "Spanish (conversational)," "German (native)."
- Do not list English.

### Computer/Technical Skills

- Only list **advanced or programming skills** (R, Python, SAS, etc.).
- **Do not list:** Microsoft Office, Google Suite, social media platforms, Lexis, Westlaw. These are assumed.
- Relevant primarily for students targeting IP or tech employers.

### Certifications

- Include if relevant to target employers. Add context if the certification name is not self-explanatory.

### Interests

- Be **specific and personal**: "distance running (4x marathon)," "cooking Ina Garten recipes," "dystopian literature" -- not "reading," "cooking," "travel."
- These are conversation starters in interviews. List only things you can discuss enthusiastically and knowledgeably.

### What NOT to Include

- Age, marital status, health, family information.
- Sharing ethnicity, religion, political views, or sexual orientation is the student's choice.

## Integrity

- **Be scrupulously honest.** Any misrepresentation may result in loss of OCS privileges and referral for disciplinary action.
- Misleading or omitted information can be as serious as false information.
- Be prepared to discuss every item on the resume in an interview.

## AI Usage

- AI may be used to brainstorm language edits, formatting improvements, or fine-tune narrative.
- AI should **not** draft the resume. All content must reflect the student's true experience and voice.
- Some employers may screen for AI-generated materials.
