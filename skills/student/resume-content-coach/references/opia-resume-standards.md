# HLS OPIA Resume Standards

This reference distills the resume guidance from the Harvard Law School Office of Public Interest Advising (OPIA). Use it alongside `hls-resume-standards.md` when the student is targeting public interest, government, or nonprofit employers.

Source: HLS OPIA resume guidance and the OCS/OPIA comparative analysis.

## When This Reference Applies

Use OPIA standards when the student is targeting:

- Legal aid organizations and public defenders
- Nonprofit legal organizations (ACLU, NAACP Legal Defense Fund, Earthjustice, etc.)
- Government agencies (DOJ, state AG offices, federal agencies, local government)
- International human rights organizations and NGOs
- Policy organizations and legislative offices
- Any employer where the hiring motivation is public service, not profit

If the student is unsure or targeting both private and public interest employers, coach them to prepare two versions of their resume -- one for each track.

## What Differs from OCS Standards

OPIA shares the "HLS Baseline" with OCS (see below) but diverges on several strategic points. These are not optional preferences -- they reflect how public interest employers evaluate candidates differently from firms.

### Page Length

- **Two pages are acceptable** if the candidate has substantial pre-law or public interest experience that is directly relevant.
- Do not pad to fill two pages. A strong one-page resume is always better than a weak two-pager.
- If the student has fewer than three years of pre-law work experience, one page is usually sufficient.

### Clinics, SPOs, and Pro Bono Work

- **Clinical and Student Practice Organization (SPO) work should be listed under Experience**, not under Education Activities. This is a major structural difference from OCS guidance.
- Clinics demonstrate hands-on advocacy, client interaction, and substantive legal work -- exactly what public interest employers value most.
- Pro bono projects and community service work deserve their own section header ("Pro Bono / Community Service") or prominent placement under Experience.

### Relevant Coursework

- **Include a "Relevant Coursework" line** under the HLS Education entry to signal substantive expertise aligned with the employer's mission.
- Example: "Relevant Coursework: Immigration Law, Administrative Law, Federal Indian Law, Child Advocacy"
- Keep it to one line (4-6 courses maximum). Only list courses that directly relate to the target employer's practice area.

### Action Verbs

- Public interest employers respond to verbs that signal advocacy, direct service, and community engagement.
- **Preferred verbs:** Advocated, Represented, Organized, Empowered, Counseled, Drafted (legislation/policy), Testified, Investigated, Mediated, Mobilized, Trained, Collaborated
- **Still strong:** Researched, Analyzed, Managed, Coordinated, Developed, Led
- **Avoid overusing:** "Assisted" and "Supported" are weak in both tracks, but especially so in public interest where employers want to see the candidate as an active agent, not a passive helper.

### Skills Section

- **Language proficiency is especially valued.** Public interest employers serving multilingual communities prioritize candidates who can communicate directly with clients.
- List community outreach tools, mediation training, trauma-informed practice certifications, or other specialized skills relevant to public service work.
- The OCS prohibition on listing Westlaw/Lexis and basic office software still applies.

### Header and Address

- A **"Permanent Address"** line can signal geographic ties to a specific community or market the student wants to serve.
- This is more strategically important for OPIA than OCS, where geographic signaling is less critical.

### The "Commitment Narrative"

- Public interest employers look for evidence of sustained commitment to public service -- not a single summer internship, but a pattern.
- The resume should tell a coherent story: coursework, clinical work, pro bono, volunteer experience, and pre-law service positions should all reinforce the same narrative.
- If the student has private sector experience, help them frame it in terms of skills that transfer to public interest work (client counseling, regulatory analysis, stakeholder engagement).

## What Stays the Same (HLS Baseline)

These rules apply identically under both OCS and OPIA standards:

- **Formatting basics:** No periods at end of bullets. Clean, simple layout. Consistent font, spacing, alignment.
- **Latin honors:** Italicized and lowercase (*magna cum laude*, *summa cum laude*). Phi Beta Kappa is NOT italicized.
- **Journal names:** Italicized.
- **Education order:** Harvard Law School first, then undergraduate institutions in reverse chronological order.
- **Degree terminology:** "Juris Doctor" not "Juris Doctorate." Consistent abbreviation style across all entries.
- **Prohibited items:** No GPA, no LSAT, no personal email, no full street address, no objective statement, no basic computer skills, no English as a language.
- **Tense:** Present for current positions, past for past.
- **No personal pronouns** in bullets.
- **Integrity:** All content must be scrupulously honest.
